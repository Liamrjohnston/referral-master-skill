# Diagnosis: why referrals aren't happening

Work through these in order. Most owners have two or three at once; name the one that matters most. Point at the facts in the brief that show it.

## 1. Never asked, or only asked passively

**Signs:** "I've never really asked." A flyer, a referral page, a programme in an email footer, a line in the newsletter. No one has made a personal ask at a good moment.

**Why it matters:** happy clients mean to refer and then forget. A passive offer relies on them remembering, at the right moment, with no prompt. A person asking, at a good moment, is what turns goodwill into an introduction.

**Fix:** personal asks, one at a time. If staff see clients more than the owner does, give the person the client trusts one simple line to say.

## 2. Nobody knows who to send

**Signs:** asks like "know anyone who needs a website?" or "tell your friends". An ideal client described as "anyone". Past referrals that were small, poor-fit or painful jobs.

**Why it matters:** "anyone" makes people search their whole network and come up blank. When they do send someone, it's whoever was nearest, not whoever is right.

**Fix:** one line that names the kind of person and the moment they'd say it out loud. Check the owner's best jobs (by money, fit and enjoyment) and describe those people. For example: "a friend who just got engaged and is starting to look at venues" (wedding photographer), "a cafe or shop owner who says their books are a mess before tax time" (bookkeeper), "someone at your gym who mentions their knee is still sore months later" (physio).

## 3. Asking the wrong people

**Signs:** plans to "email all past clients" or "text everyone". Clients in the list with an open problem, a dispute, a low score, a late payment fight, a job still in progress, or who have moved away.

**Why it matters:** asking a let-down client for a favour reminds them they were let down. Their friends hear about it. A blast also treats your best advocate the same as a stranger.

**Fix:** sort every client (see who-and-when.md). Fix first, ask later.

## 4. Asking at the wrong moment

**Signs:** the warmest moments passed without an ask (the reveal, the launch, the 10 out of 10 reply). Asks planned for the middle of a job, or months after.

**Why it matters:** people refer when the result is fresh and they're telling the story anyway. That window is days to weeks, not years.

**Fix:** tie each ask to a moment (see who-and-when.md, "Moments").

## 5. No thanks, no loop

**Signs:** past referrers never thanked, or thanked once vaguely. The referrer never heard what happened.

**Why it matters:** a referrer took a small risk with their own name. If nothing comes back, they assume it didn't matter, or went badly. Thanked and updated referrers refer again.

**Fix:** a personal thank-you that names who they sent and what happened, before any new ask.

## 6. Hidden channels ignored

**Signs:** someone whose own customers need the owner (a wedding planner, a vet, an accountant, a venue, a supplier). Several clients who know each other (same building, street, gym, industry group). A staff member who already gets referrals.

**Why it matters:** one partner can send more work than ten clients. Clusters spread by themselves once one person talks.

**Fix:** a partner note and a call. A neighbour or peer version of the ask for clusters. Copy what the staff member already does.

## 7. Nothing worth repeating

**Signs:** clients are satisfied ("fine", "quiet") but nobody mentions anything specific. Only raise this when the facts show it; don't lecture an owner whose clients are clearly delighted.

**Fix:** keep it small and practical: one memorable, repeatable detail in the service, or make the result easy to show (photos, a before and after). Don't invent one for them.

## 8. What the owner floated

Check every idea the owner brought:
- **"The reward isn't big enough."** Usually wrong. Check whether anyone was asked in person first.
- **"I'll post / blast everyone."** Usually brings vague, poor-fit referrals.
- **A claim they want to make** (spots left, client counts, results, testimonials). Check it against the facts. If it isn't true, say so in one line and give the honest version.
- **Paying for reviews or asking only for 5 stars.** Always no. See rewards-and-reviews.md.

## What a client is worth

When a reward or budget comes up, work it out from the owner's own figures and show the working:

- **One-off work:** average job value (yours or calc). If they gave a margin, kept = value x margin.
- **Recurring work:** price per visit x visits per year = revenue a year. x margin = kept a year. x average years a client stays = kept over the relationship.
- Label each figure (yours), (calc) or (estimate). If a figure is missing, write [N] and show the formula.
- Compare with the reward. If the owner has the value badly wrong, correct it in "The real problem".

Never use an industry average or a "referral customers are worth X% more" statistic unless the owner supplied it.
