# Messages

Every message is in the owner's voice and usual channel: a text for most local service clients, an email or LinkedIn message for business clients. Under about 90 words. Plain, warm, specific. It should sound like the owner wrote it on their phone, not like a template.

## The moves in a good ask

Build each ask from these, in this order. Skip a move only if the brief gives you nothing true for it.

1. **Start with them.** Their name and the real thing you did, from the brief. "Still smiling about how calm Biscuit was at her last groom" beats "I hope this finds you well".
2. **The bridge.** One honest line that makes the ask normal, and you'd value their help. Use "might" so there is an emotional exit. (Burg's referral bridge) The bridge must be true from the brief: say "most of my work comes from referrals" only if the numbers show most. Otherwise use something true, like "I'd love to work with more people like you" or "some of my best clients came from people like you".
3. **The picture.** The "who to send you" line: the visible signs of the right person and the words they say when they need you. Never "anyone". (Burg's "how to know"; Jantsch's trigger phrases)
4. **Shrink their world.** Don't ask about everyone they know; their mind goes blank. Name one or two small groups from the brief they can actually picture (their team, their building, their club, their fellow founders, the other parents at practice) and ask who in that group has mentioned it. (Burg, credited to Tom Hopkins; Hormozi: "who", never "if")
5. **Make it effortless.** Ask for a short email or group text introducing you both, not a name or a business card. Offer a two-line note they can forward. (Port; Hormozi's three-way intro)
6. **Lower their risk.** They are spending goodwill with a friend. Say how you'll look after the person: you'll reach out once, no pressure, an honest answer even if it's "not yet", and you'll never make them look bad. (Burg; Jantsch)
7. **Easy out.** "No worries if nobody comes to mind." Pressure kills referrals and relationships.

## After someone refers

The loop is where most owners drop the ball, and the popular skills skip it.

1. **Same day:** thank the referrer, whatever happens next. (Burg)
2. **Within a day:** contact the referred person yourself; don't make the referrer sell for you. Open with the referrer's name ("Sam Lee suggested I get in touch"). In Canada, the first email to a referred person is allowed under CASL's referral exemption only if it names who referred them; after that you need their consent to keep emailing. (Burg; CASL regulations SOR/2013-221, s. 4(1))
3. **About 3 weeks and 6 weeks later:** tell the referrer how it's going, with the new client's OK and without private details. (Port)
4. **If it turns into work:** a small, personal thank-you picked for them. (Burg, Port, Jantsch)

## Templates for shape only

Rewrite every one in the owner's words with facts from the brief. Never send these as they are.

**Thank-you to a past referrer**
> [Name], I never properly thanked you for sending [person]. We [what happened, true and short]. That only happened because you put your name behind me, and it means a lot. [Optional: small personal thank-you.] If you ever hear [the picture], I'd love to look after them the same way.

**The ask, after a recent win**
> [Name], [real detail of the result]. Thank you for trusting me with it. [True bridge line from the brief], so I might ask a small favour. Has anyone in [small group from the brief] mentioned [the trigger words]? If so, a quick email or group text introducing us would be amazing, and I'll look after them the same way. No worries at all if nobody comes to mind.

**When they already offered an intro or named someone**
> [Name], thanks for mentioning [person]. Would they be OK with me giving them a quick call? Or here's a note you can forward: [two lines]. Thanks for thinking of me.

**First message to the referred person (the owner sends this)**
> Hi [Name], [referrer] suggested I get in touch. [One line on what you do for people like them.] Happy to answer any questions, no pressure at all. Would a quick call this week help?

**Forwardable intro (the referrer pastes this to their friend)**
> [Friend], this is [owner], who [did what for me]. [One honest line on what they're like to work with.] Worth a chat if you're [the picture]. Their number is [N].

**Partner note**
> [Name], thanks again for sending me [who] and [who]. I'd love to make this easy for you. The people I'm best for are [the picture]. When you send someone, I'll [how you'll look after them] and I'll let you know how it goes. Could we grab 15 minutes next week so I can hear who you're seeing and whether I can send anyone your way too?

**Follow-up (once, about a week later, only if no reply)**
> No pressure on this at all, [Name]. Just bumping it in case it got buried. Thanks either way.

Then stop. One nudge, never a sequence.

**Fix-first note (no ask in it)**
> [Name], you told me about [problem] and I haven't fixed it yet. That's on me. Can I come by [day] to sort it?

After the fix is confirmed and they're happy, they can move to "Ask later".

**Honest review request (separate from the referral ask)**
> [Name], if you have a minute, an honest review would really help other people find us: [direct link]. Whatever you think, good or bad, is useful. Thank you.

No reward, no star request, sent to everyone in the group, not only the happiest.

## What never goes in a message

- A detail the brief doesn't give: an emotion, a quote, a reaction, a result, a surname. Write about what the brief says happened, nothing more.
- An outcome you haven't checked, such as telling a referrer their friend is "loving it" before the owner has asked.
- A share-of-work claim ("most of my work comes from...") the numbers don't support. Two referrals in a year is not "most"; say "I'd love more clients like you" instead.
- A thank-you that skips the outcome. Tell past referrers what came of their referral, from the brief.

- Fake urgency: "almost full", "this week only", unless it's true and in the brief.
- A guilt trip or a quota ("I need 5 referrals this month").
- "Anyone at all" asks.
- A claim the client couldn't make in their own words, or a testimonial written for them.
- A reward for a review, or a request for a specific star rating.
- A referral ask attached to an invoice, a complaint reply or a mid-job update.
