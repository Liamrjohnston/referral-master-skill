# Who to ask, and when

## The groups

Put every person in exactly one group. Write the reason from the brief next to each.

| Group | Who | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **Thank first** | Already sent someone | A real thank-you naming who they sent and what happened. Then, in the same note or the next, the clear picture of who else to send. |
| **Partners** | Their own customers need the owner (wedding planner, vet, accountant, venue, supplier, another trade) | A partner note and a short call: who to send, how you'll look after them, how you'll keep them posted, what you can send back. One partner can send more than many clients over time. (Jantsch; Port's "gang of five", no commissions) |
| **Ask now** | Happy, with a recent win or a clear signal | The personal ask, this week. |
| **Ask what would make it great** | Satisfied but not delighted (middle scores, "fine", no complaints and no praise) | "What would have made it a 10?" Fix what they say. Ask for a referral only once they are delighted. (Jantsch routes survey scores this way) |
| **Ask later** | Happy but mid-job, or the moment has passed | A named trigger that unlocks the ask (delivery, next visit, next milestone, next 9 or 10 reply). |
| **Fix first** | An open problem, complaint, dispute or low score | The fix, with a date. A check-back after. No ask until it's resolved and they're happy. |
| **Leave out** | A bad relationship, moved away, or a poor fit whose friends would be poor fits | Nothing. Say why in one line. |

## Ranking "Ask now"

Put first:
1. **Anyone who has offered an intro, asked for contact details to pass on, or named a friend who needs the owner.** That is a referral already waiting. Act today: send the details, or ask "can I call them directly?"
2. **The freshest win.** Finished in the last few weeks, praised the work, shared photos or tagged the owner.
3. **Proven sharers.** Left a review, posted about it, or talks about the owner publicly.
4. **Well-connected to the right people.** Their network matches the "who to send you" line (a practice manager who knows other practice managers, a member of a club full of similar people).

Then the satisfied but quiet, lowest priority. Older "fine" jobs rarely beat a recent delighted one.

Spend is a tie-breaker only. Kumar, Petersen and Leone found the customers who buy the most are often not the ones who refer the most.

**Referred-in clients.** Someone who came to the owner by referral already knows how it works. Their ask can say so: "someone sent you my way, so if you ever hear..." In a field test with more than 10 million referred customers, "You were referred in, now refer your friends" beat a plain invite by more than 20% (Gershon and Jiang, 2024; an app, not a service business, so treat it as direction, not a promise).

**Protect your referrers.** Referred clients are much more likely to leave after the person who referred them leaves (Van den Bulte and colleagues, 2018, at a bank). Keeping a referrer happy protects the clients they sent.

## Signals to read in the brief

- **Warm:** praise in their own words, photos or posts shared, a review, a top satisfaction score, renewals and repeat bookings, offering to pass your details on.
- **Cold or risky:** lukewarm words ("ok", "no complaints"), billing disputes, an unresolved complaint or callback, a low score, a project that ran late or over budget (even if it ended well, ask for honest feedback before a referral).
- **Out:** moved away from the service area, business closed, the relationship ended badly.

## Moments

The best times to ask, in order of strength:
1. **The reveal.** The day or week the result lands and they see it.
2. **When they praise you.** Reply to the praise with a thank-you, then the ask.
3. **After a high score.** Straight after a 9 or 10 on a satisfaction check.
4. **After a fixed problem.** Once they confirm it's fixed and they're happy, not before.
5. **A milestone.** An anniversary, a renewal, a result they can measure.
6. **When they tell you about someone.** "My sister is looking for someone like you" is the moment.

Never: mid-job, the same week as a dispute, attached to an invoice, or in a mass message.

**Plant it at the start.** With a new client, say early that most of the work comes by referral and that once they've seen results you might ask. Then ask after the result. (Jantsch: tell them at signing you'll check in around 90 days and ask then.)

**How often.** One ask and at most one light follow-up, then wait for the next win. About once a quarter per person at most.

## Has the ask been earned?

An ask is earned when there has been genuine, recent contact and a result the client can point to. Someone the owner hasn't spoken to in a year or two gets a real check-in first (something useful, an introduction for them, a "thinking of you"), and the ask comes later. (Port)

## Clusters

When several clients share a building, street, industry or group, the ask can be about that place: "If anyone else at the dog park mentions their dog hates the salon, I'd love to look after them too." It's specific, easy to picture, and often efficient for the owner. Only ask cluster members the brief shows are happy. Being in the same building or group doesn't tell you how someone feels; if you can't tell, say so and ask the owner to check before sending.

## Staff

If a staff member sees the client every week, they're the trusted person. Give them one line to say at a good moment, and tell the owner to thank them when it works. If one staff member already gets referrals, copy what they do.
